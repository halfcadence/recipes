## 2026-08-18

- **Added [Bacon]({{ '/r/bacon' | relative_url }}) (No. 135) to Sides — oven bacon started in water, written for BLTs.** 400°F on a rimmed half-sheet with ~120g of water: the pan holds at ~212°F until the water boils off, so the fat renders before the surface browns, which is what keeps the strips flat and stops the thin edges scorching. 22–30 min standard cut, 30–35 thick. Notes carry the sandwich specifics (halve the strips crosswise before baking; toast the bread in the reserved fat) and the two failure modes — a crowded pan steams, and a rack costs the even browning that frying in the rendered fat gives. No hero photo; nothing in the registry depicts it.

## 2026-08-11

- **Added [Tart Shell]({{ '/r/tart-shell' | relative_url }}) (No. 131) to Pantry — Dominique Ansel's go-to sweet shell.** Sourced to his "go-to vanilla sablé" from *Everyone Can Bake* (2020) via [The Splendid Table](https://www.splendidtable.org/story/2020/03/11/my-go-to-vanilla-sable-tart-shell) and the [Food52](https://food52.com/recipes/82970-go-to-sable-tart-shell-recipe) reproduction, which agree on quantities and the 350°F / 15–20 min blind-bake: 185g flour, 85g confectioners' sugar, 50g cornstarch, 130g butter, 1 egg, salt, vanilla. **Verifying the source corrected the brief:** the recipe was requested as an almond-flour pâte sucrée, but Ansel's canonical shell uses cornstarch, not almond flour, and is rubbed rather than creamed (a sablé) — no reputable source reproduces an almond-flour version, so the cornstarch sablé is what shipped.
  - Filed in Pantry beside [pâte brisée]({{ '/r/pate-brisee' | relative_url }}) as its sweet counterpart, and the lede/notes/Analysis are written as an explicit contrast to it. The Analysis (46.6% structure, 22.2% fat, 16.9% sugar, 11.7% liquid) shows the difference from brisée (55.4% / 24.4% / 0 / 18.0%) is *not* the fat — it's the added sugar, the cornstarch (10% of the dough, pure starch with no gluten protein), and the absence of any free water, which together is why it bakes short and sandy rather than flaky. This also settles the open question left in brisée's own note ("pâte sucrée is not this dough with sugar added").
- **Added [Lemon Tart]({{ '/r/lemon-tart' | relative_url }}) (No. 132) to Sweets — Marco Pierre White's tarte au citron from Harveys, in the tart shell above.** Filling quantities (9 eggs, 400g caster sugar, zest of 2 lemons, juice of 5 ≈ 250g, 250g double cream, 250°F bake) confirmed exactly by two independent reproductions, [The Caterer](https://www.thecaterer.com/news/harveys-lemon-tart-by-marco-pierre-white) and [BigSpud](https://bigspud.co.uk/marco-pierre-whites-lemon-tart/); a third source is a scaled-down variant and was not blended in. MPW's own oversized 500g-flour pastry was dropped in favor of composing the classic filling into the [tart shell]({{ '/r/tart-shell' | relative_url }}), the same sub-recipe pattern [Flan Pâtissier]({{ '/r/flan-patissier' | relative_url }}) uses with brisée.
  - The Analysis covers the filling (per the composite-dessert rule) and explains the signature 250°F bake: at 4.4% egg protein and **zero starch**, it is a naked egg custard that curdles above ~185°F, so unlike the starch-shielded flan (which bakes hot) it must bake low. The 30.4% sugar (vs flan's 13%) and the lemon acid both modify the set — sugar raises and slows coagulation, acid firms it — which is why a starchless citrus custard slices cleanly. Cross-linked to [purin]({{ '/r/purin' | relative_url }}) (pure egg-set) and [crème brûlée]({{ '/r/creme-brulee' | relative_url }}) (yolk-cream) as the set's neighbors.
  - **Kept MPW's classic quantity honest about capacity:** the ~1350g of filling is sized for a deep 8-inch tin (his is 3.75cm) or a 9-inch, so the recipe says to line the shell walls tall or bake the excess in ramekins rather than silently scaling the famous ratio down.
  - No hero photos for either: nothing in the registry verifiably depicts these dishes, and there is no fallback by design.

- **Added [Lemon Curd Tart]({{ '/r/lemon-curd-tart' | relative_url }}) (No. 134) to Sweets — a thin, stovetop-cooked lemon curd baked briefly in the [tart shell]({{ '/r/tart-shell' | relative_url }}).** The classic *tarte au citron* form, deliberately set as the counterpart to the [lemon tart]({{ '/r/lemon-tart' | relative_url }}) (No. 132) directly above it: where that pours a raw cream custard into a deep shell and bakes it low and long, this cooks the curd to a nappe (~170°F) on the stovetop first, enriches it with butter off the heat, then gives it only a 10–15 min set-bake in a thin blind-baked shell. Quantities from the two classic baked-curd sources, which agree on the ratio (~4 eggs : 200–225g sugar : 150–240g juice : zest of 3–4 lemons : 115–170g butter): [Smitten Kitchen](https://smittenkitchen.com/2025/02/classic-lemon-curd-tart/) (5 eggs, 225g sugar, ⅔ cup juice, 115g butter, 15-min set-bake) and [Fat Daddio's](https://fatdaddios.com/classic-french-lemon-tart-recipe/) (4 eggs + 3 yolks, 1 cup sugar, 1 cup juice, ¾ cup butter, 5–8 min bake); scaled to a clean 500g batch (3 eggs + 1 yolk, 130g sugar, 110g juice, 90g butter) that lines the [tart shell]({{ '/r/tart-shell' | relative_url }}) as a thin layer.
  - The Analysis contrasts it with No. 132 on the one number that separates them: **19.0% fat here vs. 12.2% there, almost all of it added butter.** That tart sets a raw filling by egg protein alone, so it must bake low and long; this pre-cooks the curd on the stove (sugar at 27.8% and lemon acid both holding the eggs back from scrambling at 170°F), so it goes in thin and the oven only firms the surface. Cross-links to the [Pierre Hermé](https://www.lovefood.com/recipes/77122/pierre-herme-french-lemon-cream-tart-recipe) *crème au citron* as the richer no-bake variant (roughly double the butter, blended and chilled, never baked) — noted but not the recipe, since the brief was explicitly thin and baked.
  - No meringue by design (that would make it a lemon meringue pie); finished bare or with confectioners' sugar / candied zest. No hero photo — nothing in the registry verifiably depicts this dish, and there is no fallback.

## 2026-08-09

- **Added [Pineapple Cake Tart]({{ '/r/pineapple-cake-tart' | relative_url }}) to Drafts — a Taiwanese pineapple cake (鳳梨酥) crust and filling in the open format of a Nyonya pineapple tart.** (Promoted to a published recipe on 2026-08-11 — link updated to its new location.) Untested, so it goes to Drafts with no number. Searched for prior art first and found none: recipes exist for the enclosed cake in a mold, for open-faced Nyonya tarts with a plain butter pastry, and for American pineapple shortbread bars, but nothing that puts the milk-powder shortbread and the reduced-pineapple paste together in a tart shell. So the two halves are lifted from published recipes and only the format is invented.
  - Filling anchored on [Sift & Simmer](https://www.siftandsimmer.com/taiwanese-pineapple-cakes/) (750g pineapple + 454g candied winter melon, 325°F) and [196 flavors](https://www.196flavors.com/pineapple-cake-feng-li-su/) (680g pineapple, 250g sugar, 20g filling per cake, 340°F); crust on [Jaja Bakes](https://jajabakes.com/taiwanese-pineapple-cakes/) (cake flour + milk powder + almond flour, 2:1 dough to filling at 30g/15g, 320°F) and [Oh My Food](https://www.ohmyfoodrecipes.com/taiwanese-pineapple-cake/) (30g dough / 22g filling, 320°F); the open-faced format on [MyKitchen101](https://mykitchen101en.com/nyonya-pineapple-tarts/) (~9g dough to ~8g jam, 355°F) and [What To Cook Today](https://whattocooktoday.com/open-faced-pineapple-tarts.html) (10–15g dough per tart, 350°F).
  - **The recipe's real contribution is a number the pineapple-cake sources leave as an adjective: pull the filling at 300g from 1000g of flesh.** They describe the endpoint instead of weighing it — "very thick and dry paste", "dark yellow/golden". Two *jam* recipes do publish a yield, and both are looser than this: [Bakeomaniac](https://bakeomaniac.com/delicious-easy-pineapple-jam-recipe-for-pineapple-tarts/) 350g from 850g whole fruit (~1.4:1 on flesh) and [What To Cook Today](https://whattocooktoday.com/pineapple-jam.html) "440–500 grams" from 2kg untrimmed (~2.5:1). A paste that holds a cut edge in an open shell goes further — 3.3:1. At 320g the filling is 21% water and weeps into the shell; at 280g it sets hard enough to crack at the cut.
  - **Opening the format inverts the dough-to-filling ratio, so the sugar had to come down.** A pineapple cake is ~2:1 dough to filling (30g around 15g); this tart is 315g to 300g, about 1:1, so every bite carries twice the paste. Sources add 200–370g of sugar per kg of pineapple; this adds 70, and the finished paste still lands at 64% sugar because the fruit brings ~100g of its own.
  - Analysis block covers both components separately (shell and filling), following the precedent set by [Tarte Tatin]({{ '/r/tarte-tatin' | relative_url }}) and [French Silk Pie]({{ '/r/french-silk-pie' | relative_url }}). The shell's 30.5% fat and 7.9% liquid — above [pâte brisée]({{ '/r/pate-brisee' | relative_url }})'s 24.4% / 18.0% — are why it is pressed rather than rolled, and why it needs no baking weights.

The sources disagree on almost everything — oven 350–450°F, bake 9–25 minutes, potatoes raw or boiled 30 minutes — so the notes give the number *and* the spread, and say which variable actually explains it (fillet thickness, not weight). Bedded won — the potatoes get the butter and the rendered fish fat, and the fish is lifted out of its own liquid. The note also records the one number no source supports at all: nobody publishes a liquid volume per packet except Well Seasoned Studio's 1 Tbsp, which is where the 15g comes from.

- **Corrected the shrimp timing table against published sources — the colossal row was the one number no source supports.** The flagged cell was 7–9 minutes off heat for shell-on U-15. Checked against real recipes instead of my own estimate: [ATK's Classic Shrimp Cocktail](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/7228-shrimp-cocktail) rests shell-on 16–20 for 5–7 min, [ATK's How to Not Overcook Shrimp](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/6780) gives peeled extra-large-to-jumbo 5 min covered from boiling water, [ATK's Mexican Shrimp Cocktail](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/1684) 5 min, [Food Network](https://www.foodnetwork.com/how-to/packages/food-network-essentials/how-to-boil-shrimp) ~2 min peeled with "a minute or two longer" shell-on, and [Li et al. 2020](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jfpe.13505) measured unpeeled prawns needing exactly **one** more minute than peeled. The anchors converge on 5 minutes for extra-large and jumbo, and the only measured shell penalty is a single minute — not the 2–3 the old colossal row implied. 16–20 shell-on 5–6 → **5–7** (matching the one tested published row), U-15 shell-on 7–9 → **6–8**, 21–25 head+shell 5–7 → **5–6**.
  - **U-15 head + shell now reads "probe it" instead of a time, and that cell is the real finding.** No published recipe gives an off-heat time for colossal at all — ThermoWorks abandon the clock for a thermometer at that size. A number I could not source had no business sitting in a table that looks authoritative, so the table says so and gives 140°F at the thickest part instead. A new "where these times come from" table lists every anchor with its link, and states plainly that no authoritative count-per-pound chart exists (every one that turns up in a search is machine-generated) — so the column shape is reasoned from the ATK anchors plus the measured one-minute penalty, not copied from a source that had already done the work.
  - **Two other claims of mine were wrong.** "Proteins set between 120 and 140°F" was unsourced invention; replaced with what three sources actually converge on — pull at 140°F ([ThermoWorks](https://blog.thermoworks.com/perfectly-cooked-shrimp-cocktail/)), a 145°F seafood floor ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-fresh-and-frozen-seafood-safely)), water held [155–163°F](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/features/shrimp-cocktail) by size. And "the shell is a barrier" implied insulation, which is the popular explanation and not what the literature says: a shrimp cuticle is [tens of microns](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4450593/) in front of an [~11mm](https://doi.org/10.3390/foods14030428) tail, a fraction of a percent of the meat behind it, and **nobody has published a thermal conductivity for intact shrimp shell** — a [2026 review](https://doi.org/10.3390/biomimetics11060373) calls that a genuine gap. Li et al. credit added heating distance plus the air gap between shell and muscle, and less moisture lost; McGee's reason is flavour leaching, not heat. A new section states the mechanism correctly and says the shell buys about a minute, not a wide margin of safety.
  - **Size names have no industry standard, and the article now says so with receipts.** The [American Shrimp Processors Association](https://americanshrimp.com/about-our-shrimp/shrimp-academy/) — "since these appellations are not universally defined and regulated, the numbers system works best" — and [Fulton Fish Market](https://www.fultonfishmarket.com/blogs/articles/shrimp-sizing) — "there are no industry standards for shrimp sizing names." "Jumbo" spans 13–15 to 26–30 across major sellers, a factor of two in the weight of one shrimp, and the federal [grade standards](https://media.fisheries.noaa.gov/dam-migration/part_5_grade_standards.pdf) never use the words jumbo or colossal. The matrix rows dropped their trade-name labels and are now bare counts; buy by the number on the bag.

Underdone costs 60 seconds with the lid back on; overdone costs the shrimp.

Fixed to 1.000 before the option could be judged. That option wasn't chosen, but a broken render would have made the comparison meaningless.
  - **`starters` is a new category and needed three things, not one.** The section in `index.md`, the label in the breadcrumb `replace:` chain in `_includes/header.html` (without it the crumb renders the bare slug), and the valid-values list in `steering.md`. No stylesheet change: nothing in `main.scss` is scoped per recipe category, so a new one inherits the recipe treatment.

## 2026-07-28

- **The article pages read as documents while the recipes read as designed, and the reason was measurable: an article's section head was the smallest type on the page.** It was set at `$fs-small` — **13px**, smaller than the 15px body it introduced — hung in a 211px margin column that carried nothing else, so at 1440px the left third of every article was empty and four things sat on four different left edges (title 268, standfirst 268, head 268, prose **499**). A recipe's section head is 21px/700 flush left. Articles now borrow that grammar outright: **21px head over a hairline at the reading measure, prose directly beneath it, everything on the title's own left edge.** Four left edges → **one**, at every width from 320px up.
  - **The measure did not change and was never the problem.** 36rem, 71 characters per line, before and after — 576px of prose at every tier from 700px to 2560px. What changed is that the prose no longer starts 231px inside the title. This is the opposite of the usual fix: nothing got wider, the page just stopped having a private margin column for one 13px line.
  - **The head's rule is capped at 40rem, and that cap is the whole reason this needs no ultrawide branch.** `grid-column: 1 / 10` is a *share of the field*, and an article's field is not a fixed width — it's 904px below `$split` but **1263–1824px** above it, where `.post-content` takes 19 of 26 columns beside the sticky rail. Uncapped, the hairline measured 942px at 1792 and **1363px at 2560** against the same 576px of prose: a rule two and a half times longer than the text under it stops reading as that section's head and reads as a page divider. Pinned to 40rem it lands on the standfirst's own right edge instead, so head, standfirst and prose share three left edges and two right ones at every width.
  - **`h2 + * { margin-top }` had to be reset to zero, not left alone.** That 56px lift existed to top-align the first block with a head hanging *beside* it in another column. With the head above its prose in the same column the same rule tears the first paragraph away from the head it belongs to — the offset was invisible support for a layout that no longer exists.
  - **Article table columns are all left-aligned now.** Every non-first column was right-aligned, which is correct for a figure and wrong for the last column, which in all 15 table-bearing articles is prose — "Most well-established range", "Sweet doughs go lower". Right-aligned prose is ragged on its **left** edge, so every row's sentence started at a different x and the eye had to re-find the beginning on each line. `tabular-nums` stays, so `1.8&#8211;2.2%` above `0.5&#8211;1.5%` still lines up figure-for-figure.
  - **Widening the gutter to match came for free and cost 41px of phone scroll.** Moving `padding-right: 1.5rem` from the row label to *every* column but the last inflated each table by 13.6px per interior column: no page overflow — a phone table is its own scroll box by design — but **more** scrolling inside it, `packing-caneles` 99px → 140px and `salt-in-baking` 0 → 11px. Left-alignment was the change asked for; the gutter was collateral.   - **`columns`, not a second grid.** A step list has to *balance* across the two columns (7 steps → 4 + 3) and only multicol does that without measuring in JS; multicol is also column-major, so visual order stays source order (asserted on every recipe at every width). The **reading measure does not move**: 26 tracks read as three 8-track bays (8 + 1 + 8 + 1 + 8), and one bay is 548px at 1881px — which is the 34rem every step was already capped at. The second column is free.
  - **The step number moves to the head of its step.** A right-hung number works in one column because nothing is to its right; in two, the next column's text sits 20px away and `01` reads as if it belongs to step 05. This was found by looking at a render, not by measuring — every geometric assertion passed on the version with the bug. Leading the step removes the ambiguity without spending measure on an alley (the alternative, one track of gutter between the columns, cost 34px of measure). Same convention the notes band already uses.

The one-ID-beats-any-number-of-classes trap, third time.
