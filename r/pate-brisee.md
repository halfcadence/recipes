The plain French short pastry — flour, cold butter, a yolk, ice water, salt. No sugar, so it works under a custard, a quiche, or fruit. Rubbed by hand, chilled once as a disc and again as a lined shell.

## Ingredients

| | |
|---|---|
| 200g | all-purpose flour |
| 100g | cold unsalted butter, cubed |
| 1 | egg yolk |
| 40g | ice water |
| 3g | salt |

## Steps

1. Rub the cold butter into the flour and salt until sandy with some pea-sized bits left. (Food processor: pulse in short bursts, stop while it's still uneven.)

2. Mix the yolk into the ice water, add it, and bring the dough together — don't overwork it. Flatten into a disc, wrap, and chill at least 1 hour.

3. Roll the dough 2–3mm thick. Line the ring or pan — base and full sides — pressing into the corner and leaving a slight overhang. Dock the base all over with a fork.

4. **Freeze the lined shell hard, at least 30 minutes**, before filling or baking. It stops the tall walls slumping and the base going soggy.

**Structure and fat sit where [matcha shortbread]({{ '/r/matcha-shortbread' | relative_url }}) does (51.5% / 23.5%); the water is what makes it a pastry rather than a sand.** Shortbread's 9% liquid and no free water can't develop gluten, so it crumbles; brisée's 18% develops just enough to roll thin and stand up as a 2-inch wall. Fat to flour is 1:2 — the classic brisée ratio, and the ceiling before the dough turns short enough to tear when lined.

## Notes

- **Keep the butter in visible bits.** Stop rubbing while it's still uneven — those flattened pieces are what make it flaky. Rubbed to an even paste it bakes closer to shortbread: tender but not layered.
- **Water is the one variable.** 40g is the working amount for 200g flour; flour and humidity move it by a few grams either way. Add the last of it a little at a time and stop when the dough just holds together when pressed — a wet dough shrinks and toughens.
- **The two chills do different jobs.** The disc rests the gluten so it rolls without springing back. The **freeze after lining** sets the shape, and that's the one people skip — a fridge-cold shell slumps down a tall ring in the first ten minutes of baking.
- **Blind-baking, when the filling needs it.** Not always required — a thick, pre-cooked, starch-set filling poured in hot bakes the raw base through (see [Flan Pâtissier]({{ '/r/flan-patissier' | relative_url }})). For a thin, wet, or unbaked filling: bake the frozen shell at 425°F for 25 minutes with weights, then 5 minutes uncovered.
- **Make-ahead.** The disc keeps 3 days refrigerated or 2 months frozen; a lined shell keeps a month in the freezer, wrapped, and goes into the oven straight from frozen. It's worth making the dough the day before whatever it's holding.
- **Scaling.** This lines an 8×2-inch ring with a little to spare. A 9-inch × 1-inch tart pan or a 9-inch pie plate takes the same batch; a 9×2-inch ring wants ~1.25× (250g flour, 125g butter, 50g water, 4g salt).
- **Pâte sucrée is not this dough with sugar added.** Sucrée is creamed, sweetened, and sturdier-but-sandier; brisée is rubbed and neutral. Use brisée where the filling is the sweet part.
</div>
