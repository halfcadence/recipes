Dominique Ansel's go-to sweet shell — a vanilla sablé. Where [pâte brisée]({{ '/r/pate-brisee' | relative_url }}) is flour, butter, and water rubbed to a flaky short pastry, this adds confectioners' sugar and swaps a fifth of the flour for cornstarch, so it bakes sandy and crisp rather than flaky. Butter rubbed in (not creamed), one egg, no added water. It holds a fruit tart, a [lemon tart]({{ '/r/lemon-tart' | relative_url }}), or a ganache.

## Ingredients

| | |
|---|---|
| 185g | all-purpose flour |
| 85g | confectioners' sugar |
| 50g | cornstarch |
| 3g | vanilla bean paste (or seeds of ½ vanilla bean) |
| 1g | salt |
| 130g | unsalted butter, cubed, room temperature |
| 1 | egg (~50g) |

## Steps

1. Whisk the flour, confectioners' sugar, cornstarch, vanilla, and salt together in a bowl.

2. Add the room-temperature butter and rub it in by hand (or paddle on low) until the pieces are pea-sized and the mix is sandy. Don't cream it — it should stay cool and loose, not fluffy.

3. Add the egg and mix with a spatula **just until it comes together into a smooth dough** — stop the moment it's uniform. Overworking builds gluten and toughens it.

4. Flatten into a disc, wrap, and refrigerate at least 30 minutes (up to overnight), until cold but pliable — the texture of clay. Warm dough shrinks in the oven.

5. Roll the dough 3mm thick, working quickly before it warms. Cut a round 2.5cm wider than your ring or pan so it can climb the sides.

6. Butter the ring (set it on a parchment-lined sheet) or pan. Press the dough into the base and full sides, keeping an even thickness, and trim the overhang with a paring knife. **Rechill 15 minutes** if it softened while lining.

7. **Blind-bake:** line with parchment, fill with pie weights (rice or dried beans), and bake at 350°F for 15 minutes. Lift out the parchment and weights and bake 5–8 minutes more, until evenly light golden and dry. Cool before filling.

## Analysis

<div class="analysis" markdown="1">

Total dough: **504g**

| Component | Amount | % |
|---|---|---|
| Structure solids (flour, cornstarch) | 235g | 46.6% |
| Fat (butter, egg) | 112g | 22.2% |
| Sugar | 85g | 16.9% |
| Liquid (butter water, egg water) | 59g | 11.7% |
| Egg/protein | 6g | 1.2% |

**Three things separate this from [pâte brisée]({{ '/r/pate-brisee' | relative_url }}) (55.4% structure, 24.4% fat, 18.0% liquid, no sugar), and none of them is fat — the fat is basically the same.** It's the 16.9% sugar, the cornstarch, and the missing water. There is no *free* water here: the 11.7% liquid is all bound inside the butter and the egg, versus brisée's 40g of added ice water. Sugar competes with what water there is and physically gets between the flour proteins, and the 50g of cornstarch (10% of the dough) is pure starch with no gluten protein at all, diluting the flour that's left. So almost no gluten develops — the crust bakes short and sandy (*sablé* is French for "sanded"), crisp rather than flaky, and it's rolled cold-and-fast then rechilled rather than rested to relax a gluten network it never built.

</div>

## Notes

- **Rub, don't cream.** Creaming whips air in and makes it a cookie dough; rubbing keeps it a pastry. Keep the butter and the bowl cool — if the butter smears to a paste the shell bakes greasy and dense.
- **Weights, not docking.** Unlike brisée, this shell blind-bakes under pie weights rather than fork-docked — the weights hold the sandy, low-gluten walls up while the base sets, and it has no tall wall to slump. Dock it *and* weight it if you like belt-and-suspenders.
- **Blind-bake fully for a wet filling.** For a raw liquid filling — lemon curd, a fresh-fruit-and-cream tart — take the shell to an even golden and dry (the 5–8 minutes uncovered matter). For a filling that bakes *with* the shell, like frangipane, skip the blind-bake and build it raw.
- **Cornstarch is the sablé, not a swap for flour.** Replacing it gram-for-gram with more flour gives a sturdier, chewier, more brisée-like crust; leaving it in is what makes this sandy and tender. Don't substitute it out to "simplify."
- **Chocolate variation.** Add 18g unsweetened cocoa to the dry mix (Ansel's own variation). Reduce the flour by ~10g to keep it from going dry.
- **Lemon variation.** Swap the vanilla for the finely grated zest of 1 lemon for a citrus shell under a fruit or cream filling.
- **Scaling.** This lines one 8-inch shell with a little to spare, or a 9-inch × 1-inch pan. For a deep (≥3.5cm) 8-inch tin or tall ring — the vessel a full [lemon tart]({{ '/r/lemon-tart' | relative_url }}) filling wants — use the whole batch and press the walls tall.
- **Make-ahead.** The disc keeps 3 days refrigerated or 2 months frozen; a lined shell keeps a month frozen, wrapped, and blind-bakes straight from the freezer (add a few minutes).
- **This is not [pâte brisée]({{ '/r/pate-brisee' | relative_url }}) with sugar stirred in.** Brisée is a neutral, flaky, water-rubbed pastry for custards and quiche; this is a sweet, sandy, no-water sablé. Use brisée where the filling is the sweet part, this where the shell should be too.
</div>
